Madison’s paths to medical school

Madison, here are three ways to medical school. Two go through the military and end at the Uniformed Services University in Bethesda. The third skips the military for a civilian medical school. Here’s each one, year by year, and what to do now no matter which door opens.

The short version

The civilian route isn’t the backup plan. For getting to medical school, it’s arguably the more direct one: USUHS takes civilians straight from college, and she could start there in 2030 instead of 2031.

If she’d rather be a doctor without serving, Route C skips the military entirely. In that case, the USNA application no longer makes sense: it means at least five years of service and competing for a handful of medical school slots.

The Naval Academy is a great path to being a naval officer, but only a handful of midshipmen go directly to medical school each year. Its goal is 3 to 15 out of a class of about 1,000.

If USNA says yes

Bethesda in summer 2031 at the earliest, if selected for one of the few medical school slots. Otherwise she serves as a line officer first.

If USNA says no

Bethesda in summer 2030 if admitted. She’s commissioned as an ensign when she starts, with pay and no tuition.

If she skips the military

Civilian medical school in 2030, or 2031 after a gap year. No service owed; cost depends on in-state tuition and financial aid.

Year by year

Route A: Naval Academy

Enter summer 2027, graduate May 2031

  1. Fall 2026 – Jan 31, 2027

    Finish the USNA application and nominations by the January 31 deadline. Keep Loyola grades high: every college grade goes on the medical school application later, even if she transfers to USNA.

  2. By mid-April 2027

    Most candidates hear their final status by April 15. Appointees accept or decline by May 1.

  3. Summer 2027

    Induction Day and plebe summer. She starts the full four-year program; some Loyola courses may let her validate classes.

  4. 2027 – 2029

    Take pre-med prerequisites alongside her major (many pre-med midshipmen choose chemistry). Connect early with USNA’s Premedical and Predental Advising Committee.

  5. Spring 2030

    Take the MCAT, ideally once and well prepared.

  6. Summer – fall 2030

    Apply to medical school with the committee’s approval and compete for Medical Corps service selection. Slots are very limited.

  7. May 2031

    Graduate and commission. If selected, start USUHS that summer. If not, serve at least five years as an officer, then apply later.

Route B: Civilian pre-med

Stay at Loyola (or transfer to UMD), start USUHS summer 2030

  1. 2026 – 27 · First year

    Biology and general chemistry with strong grades. Meet Loyola’s pre-health advisors. Start steady clinical volunteering.

  2. Spring 2027

    Decide whether to stay at Loyola or transfer. Transfer deadlines usually fall before USNA decisions arrive, so apply to transfer early if she wants that option open.

  3. Summer 2027

    Build clinical hours: EMT certification, scribing, or hospital volunteering.

  4. 2027 – 28 · Second year

    Organic chemistry. Join a research lab and stay with it.

  5. 2028 – 29 · Third year

    Physics and biochemistry. Take the MCAT in spring 2029. USUHS accepts scores no later than the fall test before matriculation.

  6. June – Oct 15, 2029

    Apply through AMCAS (USUHS code 821). USUHS advises applying in June. Early Decision closes August 1, with notification by October 1.

  7. Fall 2029 – spring 2030

    Secondary application, interview, and the commissioning physical. Finish every prerequisite by June 1, 2030.

  8. Summer 2030

    Graduate, commission as an ensign, complete about a month of officer training, and start medical school in Bethesda.

Backup within Route B: the Navy’s Health Professions Scholarship Program pays for a civilian medical school in exchange for service, if USUHS doesn’t work out.

Or: medical school without the military

Route C: Civilian medical school

Withdraw from USNA, finish college as cheaply as possible, start medical school in 2030 or 2031

  1. Now – spring 2027

    Withdraw the USNA application. Her spring courses are already right for pre-med. Meet Loyola’s pre-health advisors and start steady clinical volunteering.

  2. Spring 2027

    Decide Loyola or UMD based mostly on cost. Medical schools care about GPA, MCAT, clinical hours and research, not which college she went to. UMD has in-state tuition, a large research program and a neuroscience major.

  3. 2027 – 2029

    Finish the prerequisites, get paid clinical work (EMT or scribing), and stay in one research lab for years. Neuroscience research fits her interest in neurology.

  4. Spring 2029

    Take the MCAT.

  5. June 2029

    Apply through AMCAS to 20 or more MD schools, and consider DO schools too. Or take a gap year of paid clinical or research work and apply in June 2030. Many admitted students take at least one gap year.

  6. Summer 2030 or 2031

    Start medical school.

Where to aim as a Maryland resident: the University of Maryland School of Medicine is the public in-state option and her most realistic target. Johns Hopkins made tuition free starting fall 2024 for students from families earning under $300,000, and also covers living costs under $175,000. Admission there is very hard, but if she gets in, it may cost less than a state school. NYU and Albert Einstein are also tuition-free and very competitive. Federal Public Service Loan Forgiveness can forgive remaining federal loans after 10 years of payments while working for a nonprofit or public employer, which includes many academic hospitals.

Do these now, whichever route

  • Protect the GPALoyola grades follow her to every medical school application, including from USNA.
  • Get clinical experienceSteady, real patient contact matters more than a long list of short gigs.
  • Start research earlyA lab position held for two or three years reads far better than one summer.
  • Stay fit and leadUSUHS trains officers as well as doctors. Fitness and leadership count on both military routes, and leadership helps any medical school application.

About USUHS

Class size
178 students accepted each cycle.
Typical admits
Recent classes averaged roughly a 3.8 GPA and a 512 MCAT (third-party figures; check USUHS for current numbers).
Military experience
Not required. All applicants must meet commissioning standards: physical, medical, and security.
Cost and pay
No tuition. Students are active-duty ensigns paid at O-1.
Service owed
Seven years of active duty after graduation. Residency years don’t count toward it.
Age limit
No older than 36 on June 30 of the year she starts.

Dates and requirements change. Confirm every deadline with USNA, USUHS, and Loyola before relying on it. Prepared November 2026.
